Run it yourself:
what it really takes.

Self-hosting is not a cheaper API — it is a different cost curve with an engineering team attached. Start with the memory maths, because that decides whether your plan fits on one card at all.

First decision

Three tiers, and only one of them is "self-hosting"

01

Managed API

Someone else owns the GPUs, the uptime and the model updates. You pay per token and scale to zero. The default, and usually the right answer.

Cost shape: purely variable. Zero at zero traffic.

02

Private capacity

Dedicated GPUs or a private deployment on an inference platform. You get isolation, predictable throughput and data controls, without owning a cluster.

Cost shape: fixed per hour. Wastes money when idle.

03

Your own hardware

Rented pods, a colocated box, or a rack in your office. Full control over data, weights and versions — and full responsibility for everything that breaks.

Cost shape: fixed, plus the people who keep it running.

The honest checklist before you self-host

  • 1
    You have an eval suiteWithout one, every model update is a coin flip and every quantisation is a guess.
  • 2
    You measured throughput, not read itTokens per second on your prompts, your context length, your batch size. Break-even swings on this number.
  • 3
    Somebody is on callA model you host is a service you operate. There is no status page to point at.
  • 4
    You read the licenceApache 2.0 and MIT are fine. Several open-weight models in our library carry licences we have not verified.
  • 5
    You know your utilisationA GPU rented 24/7 to serve three hours of traffic is the most expensive option on this site.
