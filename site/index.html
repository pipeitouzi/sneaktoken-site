Why free tiers quietly expire

Providers publish a generous free tier to win developers, then quietly cut it once the growth target is met. The number on the pricing page is often the number from launch day — not the number you’ll actually hit today.

Three corrections from our most recent pass. All three are still circulating as current advice:

  • GitHub Models was fully retired on 30 July 2026 — and is still the first thing most searches return.
  • Cerebras ended its no-card allowance. It is now a $5 trial that needs a verified payment method and expires in 30 days.
  • Kaggle never published a weekly GPU quota. The widely-cited “30 hours a week” belongs to paid Colab plans.

So every entry here is stamped with the date it was last verified, and that date links to the page we read. If a quota changed, it gets corrected. If a tier got killed, it comes off the list rather than being left to rot.

One rule we hold to: if we couldn't confirm a number on an official page, we print unknown instead of guessing. A directory you can't trust is worse than no directory.
